The International Workshop on Eye Movements in Programming (EMIP) brings together researchers who study how people read and understand code through eye tracking. As an interdisciplinary workshop, it draws on expertise from both eye tracking and software engineering, and has been co-located with the premier conferences of each field: ETRA and ICSE.

# History

The first EMIP workshop took place in 2013 at the University of Eastern Finland, alongside the Koli Calling Conference on Computing Education. It focused on the gaze behavior of professional programmers and on systematic methods for analyzing gaze data across programming tasks. The workshops in 2014 and 2015 grew the field and the community, and the 2017 workshop at Freie Universität Berlin consolidated the community and broadened its scope.

In 2018, EMIP was co-located with ETRA for the first time, and in 2019 with ICSE. In 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic, EMIP moved online, with paper presentations, a keynote from a psycholinguist and expert discussions. In 2021 two workshops were held, both virtually: one with ETRA and an independent event on stimulus design. The 2022 workshop, with ICSE, featured invited short papers and an expert panel. Since 2023 the workshop has been held with ETRA, mostly as a hybrid event, continuing the tradition of paper presentations and community discussion.

The workshops have led to further collaborations among participants, several joint publications, and an international [distributed collection of eye movements in programming](/dataset/), the EMIP dataset.
